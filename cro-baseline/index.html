  1. Confirmed

    The stock counter is a random number.

    Every visit to Triple Magnesium Complex showed a different "Only X left in stock": 31, 22, 36 and 27 on two days, and 36, 26, 27, 44, 24, 21 and 50 on Marine Collagen. The theme file wbn-pdp.js picks a number between 21 and 50 whenever real stock is 20 or more. The page as sent by the server says 7,550 for magnesium today. A shopper who reloads sees it change, and India's 2023 dark-pattern guidelines list overstating scarcity as false urgency.

  2. Trust

    The site claims clinical proof, then withdraws it, then disagrees with itself.

    Triple Magnesium shows a "Clinically Proven" badge, then says "Claims are ingredient-based and not for the product." The collection page says the capsule cuts cramps by 43%; its own gallery says 72%. Marine Collagen gives absorption as 4x, 1.5x and 5x on one page, and wrinkles as 35%, 53% and 36% across two. The cramps claim cites a magnesium oxide study while the FAQ tells shoppers to avoid oxide.

  3. PDP

    The number careful buyers check first is hidden.

    The magnesium collection tells shoppers to choose products that "clearly state elemental magnesium content". Neither the collection nor the Triple Magnesium page does it in text: 220 mg elemental appears only on the ninth gallery photo, and the split across the three forms isn't stated anywhere. On our visit the page's own AI assistant was suggesting "How much elemental magnesium is in each serving?"

  4. Products

    A daily-habit catalogue sold one order at a time.

    None of the nine hero products has a subscription. Wellbeing says nearly half its customers return within 12 months and about 60% of website revenue comes from repeat buyers. Marine Collagen asks for 12 weeks of daily use from a 25-serving tub; the packs only show a percentage saving.

  5. Ads

    Ad traffic lands on pages that say something else, or nothing.

    The two festive creator ads we traced land on standard collection pages; /pages/festive-gift-box is live, in the sitemap and empty. Collagen ads promise "5X better" absorption and land on a collection that says "up to 4x". Sea Buckthorn and Liposomal Iron run several active creatives while their pages carry 11 and 4 reviews.

  6. App

    The app rating is a buying-experience problem, not a product one.

    Of 26 negative app reviews in the last six months, 12 are about support and 9 about delivery. The brand hasn't replied to any review on Google Play. About 255 new 5-star ratings would take Play from 2.7 to 4.0.

What this baseline can't see yet: the conversion rate. Without GA4, Shopify analytics or Clarity, we can't measure sessions, add-to-cart, checkout or purchase rates, so this read ranks problems by how directly they touch trust, price and the decision to buy, not by measured drop-off.

Checkout runs on GoKwik (its cart scripts load on every page). On GoKwik stores, Shopify's built-in conversion rate often fails to link orders back to the sessions that produced them. Before using it as the programme KPI, compare Shopify orders ÷ Shopify sessions against the built-in figure. Backlog item 0.

02 · Data health

Read this before trusting any number

What this read rests on, and where it is thin.

  1. Critical

    No store analytics in this read.

    Every figure comes from the live pages (captured on iPhone 13 and 1440px desktop), the product data the store publishes, both app stores, Meta's Ad Library or named public sources. Funnel rates, scroll reach and tap data need GA4, Shopify and Clarity (section 10).

  2. Caution

    Traffic estimates disagree.

    Similarweb shows about 1.4 million visits over three months, roughly 470,000 a month, but it excludes app traffic and usually undercounts. Revenue maths (about ₹250 Cr annual run-rate, 35% D2C) implies more than a million website sessions a month. Treat both as ranges until GA4 is shared.

  3. Caution

    Prices moved during the read.

    Pure Korean Marine Collagen was ₹1,745 on 7 October and ₹1,529 on 12 October, during the Mid-Month Sale. Prices in section 04 are from 12 October.

  4. Caution

    86 hidden offer duplicates.

    86 of the 285 products in the store's public product feed are tagged offer_product, and 85 of those are also no-index. Check that GA4 item reports and review counts aren't split across them before reading product-level numbers.

  5. Note

    On-site ratings run higher than off-site.

    Marine Collagen shows 4.74 from 571 reviews on the site, and 4.3 from 1,856 verified ratings on Nykaa (search index, 7 October). Shoppers who compare channels see both.

05 · Leaking pages

Pages that lose trust or traffic

  1. Empty

    /pages/festive-gift-box and /pages/shilajit-launch are live and blank.

    Both load, are listed in the sitemap, and show only their heading, with no products. Creator ads are already running festive angles three weeks before Dhanteras (6 November).

  2. Collection

    The magnesium collection contradicts the product page.

    43% vs 72% on cramps, 14% longer sleep vs "77% experienced restful, deep sleep", "Visible Results in 4-8 Weeks" vs the product page's 1 to 2 weeks. It tags Triple Magnesium "Cognition & Muscle", not sleep, though the ads sell it for sleep.

  3. Hidden asset

    The collection already has what the product page is missing.

    A "Best For" comparison of all three magnesiums, and a FAQ telling people with kidney disorders, those pregnant or on medication to consult a doctor. Neither appears on the Triple Magnesium page, where the decision is made.

  4. Cart

    A 9-minute "Limited Time Offer" that restarts for every visitor.

    Four fresh sessions opened the cart at 8m 59s, 8m 57s, 8m 57s and 8m 56s. In one session it kept counting through a reload. Nothing next to it says what ends at zero.

06 · Triple Magnesium PDP deep-dive (mobile)

Where the page puts what the shopper needs

iPhone 13 (664px screen), live page 10,296px tall, captured 12 October 2026. Positions are measured; how far visitors actually scroll needs Clarity (section 10).

  1. Evidence

    "Is any of this proven for this capsule?"

    A "Clinically Proven" badge and footnoted claims such as "Prevents Migraines & Headaches", directly above "Claims are ingredient-based and not for the product". Nothing connects the cited studies to the 220 mg in this capsule.

  2. Timeline

    Three answers to "when will I feel it" on one page.

    A review says "from the 1st day", the FAQ says 1 to 2 weeks, a gallery image says 4 to 6 weeks. Sleep buyers often expect magnesium to work like a sleeping pill; this page gives them no single expectation.

  3. Value

    No reason to buy two.

    Pack of 1 and Pack of 2 both save 15%, while the banner says "up to 25%" and the member price is 20% off. At ₹1,189 for 30 servings it is about ₹40 a night, and the page never says so.

  4. Risk

    Nothing near the button about returns, safety or delivery.

    Returns are accepted only for items damaged, defective or shipped wrong. "Habit-forming", "pregnancy" and "kidney" don't appear on the page. There is no delivery estimate or pincode check.

  5. Reviews

    4.9 from 782, with no 1-star reviews.

    709 five-star, 71 four-star, 2 two-star. The first reviews shown include "Good product" and "Loved it". Tagging by outcome (sleep, cramps, migraines) would make them do more work.

08 · Understanding the user

Who the Wellbeing shopper is

Built from who the pages, FAQs and ads speak to. Confirm with surveys and session recordings (section 10).

The sleep-struggler

Arrives from "Struggling to switch off at night?" and has often tried melatonin or a basic magnesium. Needs to know it works, how soon, and that she won't depend on it. Wellbeing's own Sleep Melts ads say "non-habit-forming"; the magnesium page never does.

The label reader

Knows glycinate from oxide and checks elemental dose before paying ₹1,189. The site teaches her to avoid oxide and check elemental content, then cites an oxide study and hides the dose in a photo. The likeliest buyer, and the easiest to lose.

The woman managing cramps, PMS or PCOS

The FAQs speak to her directly. She needs to know it is safe for her and what it can honestly do. The page answers "Yes" to PCOS while disclaiming its claims, and the kidney and pregnancy note sits only on the collection.

The repeat buyer

About 60% of website revenue comes from repeat customers. She reorders by habit, but there is no subscription to opt into; she re-buys pack by pack, and the packs aren't framed around the course she is on.

11 · Competitor analysis

Five magnesium product pages reviewed on 12 October 2026: Setu (the closest rival, also a triple-magnesium complex), GNC India, Swisse India, BiOptimizers and Moon Juice.

Where Wellbeing already leads

  • Review depth. 782 reviews at 4.9 on Triple Magnesium. Swisse's magnesium shows 191. The advantage starts at 54% of the page.
  • Price per night. About ₹40 a serving at ₹1,189 for 30, against Setu's ₹46 per serve. The page never says so.
  • A distinctive format. Three forms in one time-released capsule, with a working 3D prototype ready to show it.

Where competitors are ahead

  1. Setu

    Dose, timeline, guarantee and subscription, all on the page

    "300 mg elemental magnesium" and "No Proprietary Blends" in text; one timeline, "within 1 to 2 weeks of nightly use"; a 60-day money-back guarantee; subscribe and save with an extra 10%; ₹46 per serve.

  2. BiOptimizers

    Transparency as the pitch

    500 mg elemental from seven forms, compared side by side with single-form magnesiums; a 365-day money-back guarantee; guidance for people with kidney concerns or on blood pressure medication.

  3. Moon Juice

    Dose per form and a low-risk first order

    Citrate 204 mg, gluconate 77 mg; "non-habit forming"; 45-day guarantee; subscribe and save 30%; $1.03 per serving.

  4. GNC India

    Delivery and lab proof at the button

    Pincode delivery check with a COD note, and a "View latest batch report" link on the product page.

  5. Swisse India

    Honest reviews

    Shows its 3-star and 2-star reviews in a 191-review breakdown; free shipping on ₹799 and above in the header.

None of the five shows an "Only X left" counter.
